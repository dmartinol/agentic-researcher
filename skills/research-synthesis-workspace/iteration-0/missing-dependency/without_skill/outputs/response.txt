## Executive Summary

**Preliminary Recommendation: PostgreSQL**

Based on available research on performance (RT-101) and operational complexity (RT-103), PostgreSQL is the recommended choice. Both databases meet performance requirements, but PostgreSQL presents significantly lower operational risk given current team expertise.

**Confidence Level:** Medium (pending security research completion)

---

## Detailed Analysis

### 1. Performance Evaluation (RT-101 - COMPLETE)

**Acceptance Criterion:** Evaluate performance for our read-heavy workload (90% reads, 10% writes)

#### PostgreSQL Performance
✅ **Strengths:**
- Sub-10ms P95 latency with proper indexing (verified: claim_postgres_read_performance)
- Excellent performance for structured queries
- Predictable latency characteristics

⚠️ **Limitations:**
- Vertical scaling limits for single-node writes
- Performance requires proper index maintenance

**Evidence:** PostgreSQL 16 Performance Tuning Guide (official documentation)

#### MongoDB Performance
✅ **Strengths:**
- Horizontal scaling via sharding with automatic balancing (verified: claim_mongodb_horizontal_scaling)
- Good performance for document retrieval
- Better scaling path for future growth beyond single-node capacity

⚠️ **Limitations:**
- Inconsistent performance across shard keys
- Sharding requires upfront shard key design
- Poorly chosen shard keys can cause hotspots

**Evidence:** MongoDB Sharding Documentation (official documentation)

#### Performance Verdict
**Both databases meet requirements**, but PostgreSQL offers more predictable performance for the current workload, while MongoDB provides better horizontal scaling for potential future growth.

---

### 2. Operational Complexity (RT-103 - COMPLETE)

**Acceptance Criterion:** Consider operational complexity and team expertise

#### PostgreSQL Operations
✅ **Major Advantages:**
- Team has **5 years PostgreSQL experience** (strong operational foundation)
- Mature backup/restore tools (pg_dump, WAL archiving)
- Well-understood monitoring (pg_stat_statements)

⚠️ **Minor Considerations:**
- Requires manual partitioning for very large tables
- Standard operational complexity for relational databases

#### MongoDB Operations
⚠️ **Significant Concerns:**
- **Limited team expertise**: Only 1 developer with 6 months MongoDB experience
- Sharding adds substantial operational complexity (config servers, mongos routers)
- Query optimization less intuitive for SQL-trained team

✅ **Mitigation Available:**
- Atlas managed service can reduce operational burden
- Native sharding support with automatic balancing

#### Operational Verdict
**PostgreSQL presents significantly lower operational risk.** The team's 5 years of PostgreSQL experience vs. 6 months of MongoDB experience from a single developer creates a substantial knowledge gap that would increase incident response time, operational errors, and onboarding friction.

## Preliminary Recommendation

### Choose PostgreSQL

**Primary Rationale:**
1. **Operational Safety:** Team expertise alignment dramatically reduces operational risk
2. **Performance Adequacy:** Meets all performance requirements for current workload
3. **Predictability:** More consistent latency characteristics
4. **Reduced Complexity:** Lower operational overhead

**Trade-offs Accepted:**
- Limited horizontal scaling (mitigated: current workload fits single-node capacity)
- Manual partitioning for very large tables (mitigated: not needed for foreseeable user base)

**Confidence Factors:**
- ✅ Performance analysis complete and favorable
- ✅ Operational analysis complete and strongly favorable
- ⚠️ Security analysis incomplete (could change recommendation)

## Risk Assessment

### Risks of Proceeding Without RT-102

**High-Risk Scenarios:**
1. Compliance requirements mandate MongoDB (e.g., document audit trails)
2. Security posture analysis reveals critical PostgreSQL vulnerabilities
3. Encryption or audit logging requirements favor MongoDB

**Medium-Risk Scenarios:**
1. Both databases meet security requirements, but operational costs differ
2. Managed service options (RDS vs. Atlas) have different security characteristics

**Low-Risk Scenarios:**
1. Both databases have equivalent security postures
2. Security requirements are database-agnostic

### Mitigation Strategy

**If decision must be made immediately:**
1. Choose PostgreSQL based on strong operational advantage
2. Plan for potential migration if security research reveals blockers
3. Use managed service (AWS RDS) to reduce security operational burden

**Recommended approach:**
**Wait for RT-102 completion** (expected tomorrow) to make fully informed decision. The operational advantage of PostgreSQL is significant, but security/compliance requirements could be overriding factors.
