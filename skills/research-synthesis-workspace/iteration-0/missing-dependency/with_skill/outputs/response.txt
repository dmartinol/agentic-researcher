## PARTIAL CONCLUSIONS (From Available Evidence Only)

### Performance Criterion ✅ (RT-101 Complete)

**Conclusion:** Both databases meet performance requirements for the read-heavy workload.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- PostgreSQL: Sub-10ms P95 latency with proper indexing (`claim_postgres_read_performance`, source: `src_postgres_benchmark_2026`)
- MongoDB: Horizontal scaling via sharding with automatic balancing (`claim_mongodb_horizontal_scaling`, source: `src_mongodb_docs_2026`)

**Trade-offs:**
- PostgreSQL offers more predictable latency for structured queries
- MongoDB provides better horizontal scaling for future growth
- PostgreSQL has vertical scaling limits (single-node writes)
- MongoDB may have inconsistent performance across shard keys

**Qualification:** Both solutions are viable from a performance perspective for the stated 90% read / 10% write workload.

---

### Operational Complexity Criterion ✅ (RT-103 Complete)

**Conclusion:** PostgreSQL significantly reduces operational risk due to team expertise alignment.

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Team has 5 years PostgreSQL experience vs. 6 months MongoDB (1 developer)
- PostgreSQL has mature, well-understood tooling (pg_dump, WAL archiving, pg_stat_statements)
- MongoDB sharding adds operational complexity the team currently lacks expertise for

**Operational Risk Assessment:**
- **PostgreSQL:** Low operational risk - aligns with existing team skills and established monitoring practices
- **MongoDB:** Moderate-to-high operational risk - sharding complexity, query optimization learning curve, limited team experience

**Unknowns:**
- Security operational requirements (encryption key management, audit logging) - awaiting RT-102
- Compliance-driven operational constraints - awaiting RT-102
